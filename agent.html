What each plan item carries

You tap to run each new lab; a person starts every run. Each plan item is marked Cited, Hypothesis or Ask your clinician, with a dated retest. Next time you upload a lab, it checks whether the plan worked and changes it. It never diagnoses and never suggests doses or treatment changes; anything clinical becomes a question for your clinician.

Each step in full

1. Read your lab file. Pulls the text out of the PDF your lab sent and picks out the markers it knows, such as vitamin D, LDL cholesterol or homocysteine.

2. Compare with your last lab. Checks each marker against its reference range and against your previous result: in range or not, up or down.

3. Check your last plan. Did the markers you worked on move, and did you keep the habits? Each habit is kept, swapped or retired based on that.

4. Link changes to your DNA. Looks for gene variants you carry that research relates to the changed markers. A link means "related", never "caused".

5. Check the research. Searches PubMed and Europe PMC, and a model checks each paper against each claim. A habit is marked "Cited" only when the papers support it; otherwise it stays a Hypothesis.

6. Plan next steps. Small daily habits, a repeat test with a date, and questions for your clinician. Fixed rules route each item: Cited, Hypothesis or Ask your clinician.

7. Set up your plan. Puts the habits, the retest date and a short sheet for your clinician into the app. Anything that leaves the app, such as a calendar or Telegram reminder, a clinician link or a lab order (US sandbox), waits for your tap and shows what it will share.

What it never does, in full

Never diagnoses a condition or tells you what a result "means" for your health. Never sets a dose for you, or tells you to start, stop or change a medicine.

Reference: where official guidelines exist, such as NIH reference intakes, it shows them with the source, plus food sources and research context. Instead: clinical topics become neutral questions for your clinician, on a sheet you can bring along.

Every sentence the model writes passes a guard that blocks personal doses, medication changes and diagnoses; if it fails, a fixed template is used instead.

Privacy in full

The demo is synthetic. The live demo runs on a fictional person's data. Each visitor gets a private copy that is deleted after 7 days.

The model sees a coded minimum. The Lab Agent's seven steps send coded facts only to the language model (Anthropic's Claude), such as "vitamin D, low, steady" and a gene code. No name, email, exact values or file text. Zero data retention terms are requested from the provider, not confirmed.

Reading an uploaded lab is a separate call. The report text goes to Anthropic's Claude with lines we recognise as personal removed. Values, sex, age, test date and lab name stay, and the removal can miss an unlabelled name. Images are not sent.

Logs carry no content. The agent's step log records which step ran, when and how long it took. No values, no prompts.

Encrypted at rest. Health data is stored encrypted under a per-user key held by our server, which unwraps it to run the agent. Client-held keys are roadmap. Deleting your data destroys that key.

Real labs open with the free beta. Uploading your own lab results opens to beta testers in small groups, outside the EU and the UAE until a data protection review is done. The demo cannot upload files.

Older insight cards elsewhere in the app still send a fuller profile than the agent does. The table of what leaves the app is on Your data.
